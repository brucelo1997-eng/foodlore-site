FoodLore vs Cronometer: Nutrients vs Constitution

Sep 29, 2026·6 min read

Among people who take nutrition seriously — dietitians, athletes, quantified-self types — one app comes up again and again: Cronometer. It is the most precise, most honest food tracker available, famous for its verified database and genuinely excellent micronutrient coverage. If you want to know exactly how much magnesium, vitamin K, or selenium you ate today, nothing beats it.

So what could FoodLore possibly add? A whole layer Cronometer does not have: your constitution. Cronometer tells you what the food contains. FoodLore tells you how it fits the body eating it.

Complementary, not competing

Cronometer answers: "What nutrients did I get?" — the most accurate answer in the industry.
FoodLore answers: "Does this food suit my body?" — nutrients plus a TCM constitution score, with no logging required. Use one for nutrient gaps, the other for meal-level fit.

What Cronometer does brilliantly

Three things set Cronometer apart. First, micronutrients: where most trackers stop at calories and macros, Cronometer tracks dozens of vitamins and minerals against official daily reference values, with charts that make deficiencies visible. Second, data quality: its database leans on verified sources rather than crowd-sourced entries of dubious accuracy. Third, no fluff: no gamified noise, no upsell maze — just precise numbers. For anyone managing a deficiency, fine-tuning an athletic diet, or running a careful elimination protocol, it is the gold standard, and we say so without reservation.

The layer Cronometer doesn't have

But precision about the food is not the same as precision about the match. Consider spinach: Cronometer will correctly show you a superb nutrient profile — iron, folate, vitamin K, magnesium. What it cannot tell you is that in TCM terms spinach is cold in nature, and that a person with a yang-deficient, cold-prone digestion may feel chilled and bloated after a big raw spinach salad — while someone with a damp-heat constitution feels great on exactly the same meal.

The nutrients are identical. The bodies are not. Cronometer scores the food; nobody was scoring the eater — the eater's constitution, thermal tendencies, digestive strength. That is the layer FoodLore adds: the same 60+ nutrient analysis, plus a Constitution Score matching the food's thermal nature, flavor, and organ affinity to your body type from the classical nine-constitution framework.

Two bodies, one food database

Here is where the difference becomes practical. Take two people eating the same "perfect" Cronometer day: overnight oats with berries for breakfast, a big raw kale salad at lunch, Greek yogurt as a snack. Cronometer applauds — fiber, calcium, antioxidants, protein, all excellent. But one of them spends the afternoon bloated and chilly, reaching for a sweater in a warm room. In TCM terms, that person likely runs yang-deficient or damp: cold, raw, and dairy-heavy foods are exactly the profile their digestion struggles to transform. The other person feels fantastic on the identical menu.

Nothing about the food changed. The database is not wrong — the nutrients are real. But nutrition data describes the food, and the body doing the digesting is half the equation. This is why the constitution layer is not a rival to Cronometer's numbers but the missing context around them. Holistic nutrition keeps rediscovering what TCM systematized thousands of years ago: how a food lands depends on who is eating it, in what state, in what season.

How the two work together

  • Finding nutrient gaps? Cronometer. Its micronutrient charts are unmatched.
  • Deciding what to eat for lunch today, for your body? FoodLore. Scan, see both scores, decide in seconds.
  • Reacting unpredictably to "healthy" foods? FoodLore's constitution lens explains what nutrient tables cannot.
  • Hate logging? FoodLore requires none — scan when curious, no diary, no streaks.

The bottom line

Cronometer is the best answer to "what did I eat." FoodLore is an answer to the older question — the one TCM spent millennia systematizing: "what suits me?" Nutrients describe the food; constitution describes the fit. You do not have to choose between them. But if you have ever stared at a perfect nutrient report and wondered why your body disagreed, you have already felt the gap FoodLore was built to close.
