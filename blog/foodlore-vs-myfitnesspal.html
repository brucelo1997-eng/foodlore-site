FoodLore vs MyFitnessPal: A Different Question About Food

Sep 29, 2026·6 min read

If you have ever tried to eat better with an app, you have probably met MyFitnessPal. It is the most popular food-logging app in the world, with one of the largest food databases ever assembled, and millions of people have lost weight with it. It earned that position.

So when people ask how FoodLore compares, the honest answer is not "we are better." It is: we answer a different question. MyFitnessPal asks, "How much did I eat?" FoodLore asks, "Does this food suit my body?" Both questions matter. Almost nobody was asking the second one — until now.

Two different questions

MyFitnessPal: "How much did I eat?" — calories, macros, and daily totals, logged meal by meal.
FoodLore: "Does this food suit my body?" — every food scored twice: once by modern nutrition science, once against your TCM body constitution. Neither replaces the other.

What MyFitnessPal does brilliantly

Credit where it is due. MyFitnessPal's food database is enormous — restaurant dishes, packaged foods, home cooking — and its barcode scanner makes logging fast. For anyone working toward a weight goal with a calorie target, it is genuinely one of the best tools available: set a target, log honestly, watch the trend. The streaks, the charts, the community recipes — the whole system is built to keep you consistent, and consistency is most of the battle.

If your goal is "eat a defined amount," MyFitnessPal is excellent. We will not pretend otherwise.

The question MyFitnessPal doesn't ask

But here is the experience many people report: they hit their calorie target, nail their protein, stay inside their macros — and still feel off. Bloated after the "healthy" lunch. Sluggish after the perfect macro bowl. Breaking out despite eating clean. The numbers say everything is fine, but the body disagrees.

That gap is the question MyFitnessPal was never designed to ask: is this food right for me? Two people can eat the identical 500-calorie salad and have completely different experiences. In the TCM framework FoodLore is built on, that is expected, not mysterious. Foods have thermal natures (warming, cooling), flavors, and organ affinities, and bodies have constitutions that respond differently to each. An ice-cold smoothie bowl can be macro-perfect and still be a bad match for someone with a cold, damp, easily-bloated digestion — while being exactly right for someone who runs hot.

"Is this healthy?" turns out to have no stable answer. "Is this healthy for me?" does.

Where FoodLore fits in

FoodLore gives every food two scores. The Health Score comes from modern nutrition science — calories, macros, and 60+ nutrients measured against established daily reference values. The TCM Constitution Score matches the food's thermal nature, flavor profile, and organ affinity against your body type, determined by a short quiz based on the classical nine-constitution framework. On top of that, FoodLore offers AI tongue analysis — tongue observation is one of TCM's traditional diagnostic arts, and the app maps your tongue's signals into your constitution profile.

Just as importantly, FoodLore does not ask you to log everything. There is no daily diary to maintain, no streak to protect. You scan a food or meal when you are curious, and you get the fit — for your body, right now. For people who counted calories for years and still felt lost, that shift from quantity to fit is the whole point.

Who should use which — honestly

  • Working toward a specific weight goal with a calorie plan? MyFitnessPal is the right tool. It is purpose-built for that.
  • Eating "clean" but still dealing with bloating, sluggishness, or unpredictable reactions? That is a fit problem, not a quantity problem — FoodLore's territory.
  • Curious why the same food treats different people differently? FoodLore's constitution lens was built for exactly that question.
  • Want both? Plenty of people use MyFitnessPal for quantity and FoodLore for fit. They genuinely complement each other.

The bottom line

MyFitnessPal counts what you eat. FoodLore tells you whether it suits you. One tracks quantity, the other assesses fit — and modern holistic nutrition keeps rediscovering what TCM systematized thousands of years ago: the eater matters as much as the food. If you have ever hit every number and still felt off, you already know which question you have been missing.
