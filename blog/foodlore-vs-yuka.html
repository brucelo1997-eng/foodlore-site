FoodLore vs Yuka: Beyond the Barcode

Sep 29, 2026·6 min read

If you have ever stood in a supermarket aisle scanning a cereal box with your phone, you have probably used Yuka. The French-born app has become a shopping staple across Europe and beyond, rating packaged foods (and cosmetics) with a simple color score and flagging the additives hiding in ingredient lists. It changed how millions of people shop — and deservedly so.

So how does FoodLore compare? The short answer: Yuka tells you what's in the package; FoodLore tells you whether the meal suits your body. One audits products. The other reads you. If you have ever wondered why a "green-rated" snack still leaves you feeling off, you already sense the gap.

Two different questions

Yuka: "What's in this package?" — scan a barcode, get a rating on nutrition, additives, and organic status.
FoodLore: "Does this food suit my body?" — scan any meal, barcode or not, and get two scores: modern nutrition science plus a TCM constitution score matched to your body type. Same food, different bodies, different answers.

What Yuka does brilliantly

Credit where it is due. Yuka's additive database is genuinely useful: it names the emulsifiers, colorings, and sweeteners that manufacturers bury in fine print, and it suggests cleaner alternatives when a product scores poorly. For anyone trying to cut ultra-processed foods or avoid specific additives, it is one of the best tools in the grocery store. It also deserves credit for independence — it doesn't take money from brands to improve their scores, which is rarer than it should be.

If your question is "is this packaged product clean?" Yuka answers it well. We won't pretend otherwise.

The limits of the barcode

But here's what the barcode can't tell you. Most of what actually shapes your health never comes in a package: the home-cooked dinner, the restaurant meal, the market vegetables, the soup your grandmother made. None of it has a barcode, and Yuka has nothing to say about any of it.

There's a deeper limit, too. A Yuka score is the same for everyone. A granola bar is green-rated for you, for me, and for your neighbor — regardless of our bodies. But two people can eat the identical "excellent" product and have completely different experiences: one energized, one bloated. In the TCM framework FoodLore is built on, that's expected, not mysterious. Foods have thermal natures (warming, cooling), flavors, and organ affinities, and the nine body constitutions respond to each differently. A raw, ice-cold salad bowl can be additive-free and macro-clean and still be a poor match for someone with a cold, damp, easily-bloated digestion — while being exactly right for someone who runs hot.

"Is this product clean?" has a stable answer. "Is this food right for me?" doesn't — and that's the more useful question.

Where FoodLore fits in

FoodLore scans barcodes too — but it doesn't stop there. Point your phone at any meal, packaged or not, and you get every food scored twice. The Health Score comes from modern nutrition science: calories, macros, and 60+ nutrients measured against established daily reference values. The TCM Constitution Score matches the food's thermal nature, flavor profile, and organ affinity against your body type, determined by a short quiz based on the classical nine-constitution framework. On top of that, FoodLore offers AI tongue analysis — tongue observation is one of TCM's traditional diagnostic arts, and the app maps your tongue's signals into your constitution profile.

And there's no diary to maintain. Yuka checks the product; FoodLore checks the fit — scan when you're curious, get the answer for your body, move on. For people who already buy clean products but still wonder why some "healthy" meals don't sit right, that shift from what's in it to is it for me is the whole point.

Who should use which — honestly

  • Shopping for packaged groceries and want to dodge additives? Yuka is the right tool. Keep scanning those barcodes.
  • Eating mostly home-cooked or restaurant food? No barcode exists — FoodLore's photo scanning covers the other 80% of your plate.
  • Buying clean products but still dealing with bloating, sluggishness, or unpredictable reactions? That's a fit problem, not a product problem — FoodLore's territory.
  • Want both? They genuinely complement each other: Yuka audits the package, FoodLore reads the match.

The bottom line

Yuka rates the product. FoodLore rates the product for you — and the home-cooked meals, restaurant plates, and fresh foods that never had a barcode to begin with. Modern holistic nutrition keeps rediscovering what TCM systematized thousands of years ago: the eater matters as much as the food. If you've ever bought the cleanest product on the shelf and still felt off, you already know which question you've been missing.
